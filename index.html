How do schoolwork pressure and teacher support vary as students get older?

Across the participating countries in this dataset, reported schoolwork pressure tends to increase with age, while the proportion reporting significant teacher support tends to decrease. As students get older, are their school experiences balancing academic demands with the support they need?

beyond academic pressure

What role might social experiences at school play in students' well-being?

Every year, one in three students encounters bullying and physical abuse, and over 246 million children are victims of gender-based violence in or near schools. Peer violence at school is reported by half of all adolescents worldwide.

Research by Human Rights Watch across more than 15 countries has documented school-related violence, including physical punishment, sexual abuse and harassment, exploitation, and bullying, by the hands of teachers, school officials, and other students.

If children can't feel safe at school, their primary environment to exist in outside of a home setting, where else can they go?

According to a Yale University national survey, 58% of high school students report feeling chronically tired. The majority of teenagers are critically sleep deprived due to early school start hours, late-night homework, and screen time.

Sleep deprivation and neglected health can have serious long-term consequences for students' mental and physical health, including increased risk of depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation, and impaired cognitive function, attention, and memory.

why does any of this matter?

Based on CDC research, 49% of high schoolers feel stressed "most or all of the time" due to academics, and 40% of high school students experience persistent feelings of sadness or hopelessness. These statistics are dire; if students across the world are feeling so massively unsupported by their schools, does this not call for a major change in our systems?


This data visualization project highlights the need for a more holistic approach to education that prioritizes students' well-being alongside academic achievement. Sacrificing the well-being of our youth is not worth the cost of academic success.
